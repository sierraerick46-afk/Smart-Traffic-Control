Problem statement

Many urban traffic lights still follow fixed-time plans: the same cycle repeats whether an approach is empty or overflowing. Real demand is not constant. It changes with the hour of the day, the day of the week and the type of moment (off-peak, moderate, peak). A cycle tuned for one moment is inefficient in another, so vehicles wait longer than necessary, green time is spent on empty approaches, and drivers lose time and fuel.

Guiding question: how can an intersection in Chía adapt its green times to the traffic it actually receives, without testing unproven strategies on real infrastructure?

Justification

  • The data already exists. Hourly vehicle counts and a traffic-signal inventory can reveal recurring patterns that a fixed plan ignores.
  • Each part of the problem has a matching AI technique. Unsupervised learning identifies the type of moment, supervised learning forecasts demand, and reinforcement learning makes the control decisions.
  • It is safe by design. The agent learns in a simulator and is compared with a fixed-time baseline before anything is proposed for the street.
  • It is modular. The layered architecture (Controller – Service – Model) keeps every part testable and easy to extend.

Scope: this is a simulation-based prototype. It does not control real traffic signals.

Work plan

  1. Data preparation. Clean the dates, extract hour and day of the week, and merge the inventory with the traffic counts by intersection ID.
  2. Group. K-Means (k = 3) on the hourly profile of each day-hour slot (mean and variability of vehicles). Clusters are ordered as off-peak, moderate and peak.
  3. Predict. Features: hour, day of the week, weekend flag, cluster and a cyclic hour encoding. An 80/20 split; Random Forest and MLP are trained in parallel and scored with MSE, MAE and R² on the same test set.
  4. Decide. A simulated intersection with four approaches and two phases: random arrivals driven by the predicted demand, minimum green, yellow time and queue limits. The DQN receives queues, phase, time in phase, cluster and forecast, and chooses to keep or change the green. The reward penalizes queues and unnecessary switching and rewards vehicles served. Training uses experience replay, a target network and epsilon-greedy exploration.
  5. Evaluate and deploy. The trained agent is compared with a 30-second fixed-time plan on the same arrivals (average queue, vehicles served and vehicles lost). The app runs on Flask with gunicorn and is deployed on Render.
